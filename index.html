THE JOURNEY

Launch & initial Earth orbit

Educational scale: Orion, Earth, and the Moon are enlarged independently. The existing Earth–Moon spacing and orbital animations are preserved. Orion’s NASA-inspired route is compressed and turns with the accelerated lunar orbit; it is an educational adaptation, not an exact ephemeris reconstruction. The 120-second replay uses uneven chapter pacing, not real-time motion. Camera and chapter buttons preserve playback. Pause mission holds only the Orion replay; Earth and the Moon keep moving.

Mission guide, sources & scale

A crewed test flight

Artemis II tested Orion’s systems with four people aboard: Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen. A lunar flyby passes the Moon once; an orbital mission remains bound around it, and a landing descends to its surface.

Orion & SLS

The SLS rocket launches Orion. Orion’s crew module carries the astronauts; its European Service Module supplies power, propulsion, and essential consumables. Only the crew capsule returns through Earth’s atmosphere. This original procedural model has no powered-coast flame.

Trajectory interpretation

The storytelling follows NASA’s preflight mission map. Flight segments use an interpolated NASA/JSC OEM created 10 April 2026, including its forecast tail. Launch before the file starts and descent after entry are illustrated. This is not live tracking or a reconstruction of every manoeuvre.

Independent spacecraft replay

The Solar System controls Earth and Moon motion at all times. Mission progress and speed affect only Orion. Global Pause holds the entire simulation. The Earth-relative route turns with the original lunar orbit to illustrate a moving-Moon encounter; it is not a prediction of spacecraft positions. Switching Artemis off leaves Earth and Moon exactly where they are.
